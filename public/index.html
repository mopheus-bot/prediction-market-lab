Connecting…
Opening the WebSocket to Kalshi. This usually takes a few seconds.
On Kalshi, every market is a binary question: YES resolves at 100¢, NO at 0¢. The bot buys YES or NO contracts at a market price, then either sells before resolution or holds until the market settles. This badge is the live state: streaming (reading the orderbook), paused (won't trade but WS is open), killed (panic mode, all trades blocked), or error (WS can't connect — usually a relay issue).

The model registry tracks which ML model is active per vertical. None of these need operator action — they all just run.

Trading mode paper
On Kalshi, a "fill" means the bot bought (or sold) a YES/NO contract at a market price. paper = log only, no real money. real = place actual orders on Kalshi's exchange with your $9.73 account. off = no new trades; the WS keeps streaming so you can see state. Kill switch is the panic button — flips the bot into a state where every new trade is rejected with reason "kill_switch_active".
panic-stop: refuses all new trades immediately. No auto-close of open positions.
Caps & today
Hard limits on Kalshi exposure. per-trade cap = max cents the bot will deploy on a single fill (default 25¢ — about 1 share at 25¢, or 0.5 shares at 50¢). Daily cap = max cents deployed in any 24h rolling window (default 150¢). The bot refuses every trade that would push it over either cap, regardless of model confidence. You can override per-vertical via /start (e.g. cap politics at 5¢/trade while keeping 25¢ on weather).

Open positions
Markets where the bot is currently holding a YES or NO position. On Kalshi, a position has an entry price (what the bot paid) and a "mark" price (the current mid-market, which is what you'd get if you sold right now). Unrealized PnL = (mark - entry) × size for YES, or (100 - entry - mark + 100) × size for No, capped at the resolution value.
